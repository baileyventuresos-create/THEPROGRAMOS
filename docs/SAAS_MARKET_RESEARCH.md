## Executive summary

1. **The market prices the coach, not the athlete.** In every coach-platform competitor (TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr, TrueCoach, CoachRx, Trainerize, Everfit, BridgeAthletic), the coach or organization pays and attached athletes use the app free. Direct-to-athlete paid tiers exist only where the product is sold as content/programming to the athlete (TrainHeroic Marketplace programs at $15–$40/mo set by the coach; TheBigs.AI athlete plan billed via App Store) or as a human coaching service ($50–$397/mo — services, not SaaS).
2. **The dominant pricing unit is the active-athlete band, not the seat.** Coaches buy a flat monthly price for a roster cap (5 / 20 / 50 / 150 / 250…). True per-athlete metering exists (Kahunas per-seat; CoachRx $1.25 overage above 150; TheBigs $4–$6 per athlete for teams) but band pricing is the norm because it matches how coaches think about rosters.
3. **Entry coach pricing clusters at $19–$30/mo for ~5 clients; the working-coach tier clusters at $69–$79/mo for 20–50 clients; team/org pricing clusters at $900–$2,800/year flat.** Annual billing is universally discounted 10–17% (most commonly "2 months free" = pay 10× monthly).
4. **Free tiers are rare and small; 14-day trials are standard.** Only Everfit (5 clients forever), Trainerize (1 client forever), and TeamSnap (15 players, ads, no training features) offer permanent free tiers. Everyone else runs a 14-day no-card trial (BridgeAthletic: 30 days).
5. **Nobody owns assessment.** CoachRx includes Body/Move/Work assessments on every plan; BridgeAthletic includes FMS screening (600+ movements, coach-scored) from its $49 tier; TeamBuildr includes questionnaires and max/PR tracking with an AMS add-on at $50/mo; Teamworks AMS is enterprise quote-only. **No competitor sells a standardized, named assessment battery on a fixed reassessment cycle as the headline product.** That is The Program's open wedge.
6. **Sport verticals are marketing segments, not price segments.** No competitor charges different SaaS prices for baseball vs. soccer vs. basketball. The one segmentation found — Volt charging high schools $900/yr and colleges $1,200/yr for the identical 30-athlete cap — reads as price discrimination by budget, not by sport, and creates visible confusion. Recommendation: one price, vertical content packs included.

## 2. Competitor-by-competitor pricing evidence

### 2.1 TrainHeroic — team S&C + coach marketplace

- **Model:** Coach pays by athlete-cap band. Athletes free when attached to a coach. Separate Marketplace where coaches sell programs to athletes.
- **Coach plans (monthly only; no annual price published):** 1 athlete $9.99 · ≤5 $17.99 · ≤15 $34.99 · ≤25 $44.99 · ≤35 $59.99 · ≤50 $74.99 · ≤100 $99.99 · ≤250 $159.99 · ≤500 $199.99 · ≤750 $299.99 · ≤1,000 $399.99 · >1,000 contact sales. Assistant coaches +$9.99/mo each.
- **Per-athlete at cap:** $3.60 (5) → $2.33 (15) → $1.50 (50) → $1.00 (100).
- **Marketplace economics:** Coach pays $9.99/mo + $1/athlete/mo + 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction; athletes pay the coach-set program price, typically **$15–$40/mo**.
- **Trial:** 14 days, no credit card. No permanent free coach tier.
- **Assessment:** Performance tracking, leaderboards, compliance dashboards. No standardized assessment battery; testing is whatever the coach programs (1RM, PRs).
- **Sources:** Primary — https://www.trainheroic.com/coach/ (fetched 2026-11-20; pricing table rendered in full). Corroboration — Kahunas pricing analysis checked 2026-11-06; FitBudd TrainHeroic cost guide (Marketplace $15–$40/mo figure).

### 2.2 TeamBuildr — team strength & conditioning (schools, colleges, facilities)

- **Model:** Flat per-organization price by athlete-profile cap. **Unlimited coaches on every tier** — a key differentiator vs. TrainHeroic's per-assistant fee.
- **Strength plans:** Silver $90/mo ($900/yr) ≤50 athletes · Gold $150/mo ($1,500/yr) ≤250 · Platinum $200/mo ($2,000/yr) ≤500 · Platinum Pro $280/mo ($2,800/yr) ≤1,000 · >1,000 custom enterprise. Annual = **10× monthly (2 months free)**.
- **Add-ons:** AMS (athlete management) +$50/mo. TeamBuildr OS (gym management) $200/mo separate product. Custom app icon reported at $250/yr by third parties.
- **Per-athlete at cap (annual):** Silver $18.00/yr ($1.50/mo) · Gold $6.00/yr · Platinum $4.00/yr · Platinum Pro $2.80/yr. At a realistic 30-athlete roster on Silver: $30/athlete/yr.
- **Trial:** 14 days, no card. **No free tier** — the $90 floor means a 5-client coach pays $18/client/mo.
- **Assessment:** Max/PR tracking, wellness questionnaires on all tiers; force-plate/VBT integrations and AMS data at higher tiers / add-on. No standardized battery or fixed reassessment cycle.
- **Sources:** Primary — https://www.teambuildr.com/pricing (fetched 2026-11-20; FAQ confirms $90–$280 range, 10× annual, AMS $50/mo, OS $200/mo, unlimited coaches). Tier-by-tier caps corroborated by Kahunas (checked 2026-11-14), iTechGuides (checked 2026-09-30), Softwares.com (checked 2026-10-25).

### 2.3 TrueCoach — 1:1 online coaching

- **Model:** Coach pays by active-client band. Clients free ("100% free for your clients"). Payments built in via Stripe.
- **Plans:** Starter $29.98/mo monthly / $26.34/mo annual (≤5 clients) · Standard $69.98 / $57.99 (≤20) · Pro $164.98 / $136.99 (≤50) · >50 custom (email sales). Annual = 1 month free (Starter) / 2 months free (Standard, Pro). Yearly totals: $316.08 / $695.88 / $1,643.88.

- **Trial:** 14 days, no card. No free tier. 90-day money-back guarantee on first-time annual plans.
- **Assessment:** Custom metrics, habit/nutrition tracking, progress graphs. No standardized battery.
- **Sources:** Primary — https://truecoach.co/pricing/ (fetched 2026-11-20; page confirms 14-day trial, USD, >50 = email sales, features per tier; **the numeric price cards did not render in fetched text** — see §8). ### 2.4 CoachRx (OPEX) — individual-design coaching, assessment-forward

### 2.11 TheBigs.AI — direct-to-athlete baseball (closest D2C comparable)

- **Model:** Hybrid — athlete pays directly (App Store), coaches free, teams pay per athlete seat. The only competitor found that prices **all three** of The Program's segments in one product.

- **Team side:** Coach Hub free (roster, calendar, messaging, compliance; unlimited assistants) · Team Seats **$6/athlete/mo, $5 above 30, $4 above 60, minimum 10 seats**; pay by 5-month season or year (12 months for price of 10). A 25-athlete roster = $150/mo or $1,500/yr.
- **Relevance:** (a) The free-athlete pattern (1 real training day/week forever) is a proven acquisition mechanic in exactly The Program's youth-sport world. (b) $4–$6/athlete/mo is the market-clearing per-athlete price when an organization pays. (c) "Coaches never pay for the dashboard" is a deliberate wedge against TrainHeroic/TeamBuildr — worth noting, not necessarily copying (see §6).
- **Sources:** Primary — https://www.thebigs.ai/pricing (fetched 2026-11-20; team-seat figures rendered in FAQ).

### 2.12 Reference points (not direct competitors)

- **Kahunas** (per-seat coaching platform): $5.00/seat/mo (3–24 seats) scaling to $0.50 (500+), 20% off annual. Evidence that pure per-seat metering exists but is the minority model.
- **Optimal Athlete Teams:** $299/mo (≤30) → $599/mo (101–150) per sport — premium per-sport team pricing, 3–4× TeamBuildr. Evidence the top of the team market tolerates ~$300–$600/mo when individualization is the pitch.
- **FirstWIN Sports:** $149–$399/team/year bundled ops+assessments (psychometric, capped counts) — evidence that "assessments included" is becoming a bundle line item in youth-sports ops software, but capped and non-athletic.
- **Standalone assessment services (not SaaS):** combine/testing services charge $99–$499 per athlete event (NAHL/Next Testing) and $229–$259 per battery session (Avos Performance Battery). These are service prices — they prove parents/orgs pay real money for a single standardized testing event, which supports pricing the *system* (battery + cycle + tracking) as a premium feature rather than a free throw-in. They are **not** SaaS price anchors.

## 3. Segment analysis — who pays, what they need, what's unmet

### 3.1 Remote athlete (direct-to-athlete; payer = athlete or parent)

- **Who:** Youth through adult athletes never physically coached by The Program; parents are the actual payer for youth.
- **What incumbents offer them:** Almost nothing direct. Coach platforms treat the athlete as a free rider on the coach's subscription. The athlete-facing paid options are (a) marketplace programs ($15–$40/mo, coach-set, no assessment), (b) sport-specific D2C apps (TheBigs pattern: free sampler + paid daily plan), (c) human remote-coaching services ($50–$397/mo — a service market, not software).
- **Unmet need:** A credible, structured development path without a local coach — specifically *knowing where they stand* (assessment) and *what to do next* (prescribed movement work), with progress proof over time. Parents in particular buy visible progress.
- **Willingness-to-pay evidence:** $15–$40/mo for marketplace programs; consumer fitness-app norms $10–$20/mo. The Program's assessment cycle (test → train → retest in 28 days) is a stronger retention loop than a static program PDF — supports the top of the consumer range, not above it.

### 3.2 Independent coach (payer = coach)

- **Who:** Solo S&C coaches, private trainers, small academies (1–3 coaches, 5–50 athletes). The volume segment of this market.
- **What incumbents offer:** Programming builders, exercise libraries (3,000+ videos), messaging, payments. Pricing pain points documented across reviews: (a) band cliffs (client #6 on CoachRx jumps $29→$79; client #21 on TrueCoach jumps $69.98→$164.98), (b) add-on stacking (Trainerize), (c) payment-fee skims (TrueCoach 5%, CoachRx 2% + Stripe), (d) per-assistant-coach fees (TrainHeroic $9.99 each).
- **Unmet need:** A reason to choose *this* platform that isn't "another workout builder." Coaches serving youth athletes specifically lack (a) a defensible, repeatable assessment to show parents progress, (b) age-appropriate movement content that isn't barbell programming, (c) a development narrative (test → train → retest) they can sell. Every incumbent's library is adult-gym content; none is built around coach-led movement games for youth athletic development.

### 3.3 Team / Academy / Organization (payer = org)

- **Who:** School programs, clubs, travel orgs, private academies (50–1,000 athletes, multiple coaches/teams).
- **What incumbents offer:** TeamBuildr/Volt flat annual team pricing ($900–$2,800/yr); TeamSnap for ops (~$130–$165/team/yr, multiplies per team); Teamworks AMS at enterprise quote for elite programs only.
- **Unmet need:** (a) One org contract instead of per-team or per-coach multiplication; (b) **assessment compliance across teams** — did every athlete get tested this cycle, and how did the roster move? No mid-market tool answers this; Teamworks does, at enterprise prices with enterprise sales cycles; (c) a development system a non-S&C-specialist sport coach can run (the battery + games are coach-administered without a strength coach on staff).
- **Willingness-to-pay evidence:** $900–$2,800/yr is the established flat band (TeamBuildr, Volt). Per-athlete math at real rosters: $1.50–$3.33/athlete/mo. TheBigs' $4–$6/athlete/mo shows orgs will pay more per athlete when the product includes daily athlete-facing value, but that model gets expensive at academy scale (300 athletes × $5 = $1,500/mo) — flat bands win for orgs.

### 3.4 Segment summary

| Segment | Payer | Market-clearing price | Incumbent gap The Program fills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remote athlete | Athlete/parent | $10–$20/mo (consumer SaaS); $15–$40 marketplace programs | No assessment-driven path without a local coach |
| Independent coach | Coach | $29/mo entry · $69–$79/mo working tier | No youth-appropriate assessment + development narrative |
| Team/Academy | Organization | $900–$2,800/yr flat · $1.50–$3.33/athlete/mo | No mid-market assessment-compliance + development system |

**Patterns that matter for The Program:**

1. **Bands beat seats** for coaches (predictable bill, matches roster thinking). Pure per-seat (Kahunas) is the exception.
2. **"2 months free" annual (pay 10× monthly)** is the market-standard annual offer (TeamBuildr, TheBigs teams, TrueCoach Standard/Pro). Discounts deeper than ~17% are not observed; shallower (Trainerize 10%) reads as stingy.
3. **Payment-fee skims are a vulnerability to exploit.** TrueCoach's 5% and CoachRx's 2%+Stripe are documented coach complaints. Passing through Stripe at cost (2.9% + $0.30) with **no platform percentage** is a clean, honest differentiator — BridgeAthletic already advertises "no platform commission."
4. **Unlimited coaches is becoming table stakes** (TeamBuildr, TheBigs). Charging per assistant coach (TrainHeroic) is the legacy model.
5. **Assessment is included, never sold separately** — where it exists at all (CoachRx all plans; Bridge FMS from entry tier; TeamBuildr questionnaires all tiers; only the deep AMS layer is a $50/mo add-on). The Program should include the battery on every paid tier and differentiate on *what the assessment drives*, not on access to testing itself.
6. **Band cliffs are the #1 pricing complaint.** Soften them: generous band widths and a cheap per-athlete overage ($1.25–$2.00, per CoachRx) instead of a forced tier jump.

## 5. Sport verticals — how competitors segment (and don't)

- **No competitor prices by sport.** TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr, TrueCoach, CoachRx, Trainerize, Everfit, BridgeAthletic all sell one product at one price regardless of whether the coach trains baseball, soccer, or basketball athletes. Sport appears as *content* (Volt: 100+ sport-specific programs at Advanced tier; Bridge: sport templates) and *marketing pages* (Bridge publishes per-sport landing pages, e.g., adventure racing), never as a price axis.
- **The one segmentation found is by level/budget, not sport:** Volt charges high schools $900/yr and colleges $1,200/yr for the identical 30-athlete cap. Third-party analysis flags this as confusing ("the same plan and the same roster cap cost $900 on one page and $1,200 on another").
- **Sport-specific D2C is the exception that proves the rule:** TheBigs is baseball-only and prices per athlete seat — vertical focus is its *product* strategy, not a pricing tier.
- **Implication for The Program:** Baseball/softball, soccer, basketball, and general athletic development should be **content packs and landing pages inside one price**, not separate SKUs. Vertical-specific pricing would fragment the offer, complicate billing, and has no market precedent to anchor to. If a vertical later proves higher willingness-to-pay (e.g., travel-baseball parents), the lever is a premium *content* add-on, not a different platform price — and that should only follow evidence, not precede it.

## 6. Recommended subscription structure for The Program

### 6.1 Design principles (each traced to evidence)

1. **Coach/org pays; attached athletes are free** (§2.1–2.7 — universal). The athlete only pays when there is no coach (remote D2C).
2. **Band pricing with soft edges** (§4.6): wide bands + $1.50–$2.00/athlete overage instead of cliff jumps.
3. **Annual = 10× monthly** everywhere (§4.2 — market standard).
4. **Assessment included on every paid tier** (§4.5 — CoachRx/Bridge precedent). The battery is the product's identity; gating it would contradict the positioning.
5. **No platform percentage on payments** — Stripe pass-through only (§4.3 — exploit TrueCoach 5% / CoachRx 2%+Stripe).
6. **Unlimited coaches at org tier; generous coach counts below it** (§4.4).
7. **A real free athlete tier as the acquisition engine** (§2.11 — TheBigs pattern), because The Program's growth loop is athlete → parent → coach/team.

### 6.2 The tiers

#### Athlete Free — $0 forever
- 1 full training day per week (rotating selection from the 130 movement games)
- Daily readiness check-in; streaks
- View own assessment results and progress chart **if** a coach has tested them (read-only)
- Team schedule/messaging if attached to a team
- **Limitations (meaningful by design):** no full library access, no self-scheduled reassessment, no coach messaging, no downloadable reports, no sport-pack content
- *Rationale:* Mirrors TheBigs free pattern (proven in youth baseball). Costs ~nothing to serve; every free athlete is a lead for Remote (parent) or a roster member pulling a coach/team in.

#### Athlete Remote — $14.99/mo · $149/yr
- Full 130-game movement library with structured weekly programming (self-directed progression)
- Self-administered assessment protocol on the 28-day cycle with video submission for verification; full progress history and charts
- Sport-pack content (baseball/softball, soccer, basketball, general) included
- Family add-on: +$7.99/mo per additional athlete (up to 3 total) — modeled on TheBigs Family pattern
- *Rationale:* Sits inside the $15–$40 marketplace-program band's floor and the $10–$20 consumer-app norm; the 28-day retest loop justifies $14.99 vs. a $9.99 commodity app. Deliberately **not** anchored to the $149 in-person membership — remote SaaS and in-person coaching are different products at different price points, and conflating them would either overprice the app or underprice the coaching.

#### Coach Starter — $29/mo · $290/yr
- 1 coach · **up to 15 active athletes** · overage $2.00/athlete/mo (or auto-upgrade prompt)
- Full 8-test battery + 28-day reassessment cycle · full movement-game library · athlete app included (athletes free)
- Messaging · progress reports (parent-ready) · assessment history per athlete
- 14-day free trial, no card
- *Rationale:* $29 is the exact entry point of TrueCoach ($29.98/5 clients) and CoachRx ($29/5). Offering 15 athletes at that price ($1.93/athlete at cap) undercuts both on value while holding the market's entry price — the challenger move. Everfit's $49/20 and TrainHeroic's $34.99/15 bracket it from above.

#### Coach Pro — $79/mo · $790/yr
- Up to 3 coaches · **up to 50 active athletes** · overage $1.50/athlete/mo
- Everything in Starter, plus: group/team programming, leaderboards, custom forms/check-ins, payments via Stripe at cost (no platform %), logo/colors branding in athlete app, assessment-compliance view (who's due for retest)
- *Rationale:* $79/50 is CoachRx's exact working tier ($1.58/athlete at cap) and Trainerize's Pro 30 base. TrueCoach charges $164.98 for the same 50 — The Program at $79 with the assessment system included is a direct, evidence-based undercut of the most expensive incumbent without racing to the bottom.

#### Team / Academy — $199/mo · $1,990/yr
- **Unlimited coaches** · up to 150 athletes across multiple teams · overage $1.50/athlete/mo
- Everything in Pro, plus: multi-team rosters, org dashboard, **assessment compliance reporting across all teams** (the mid-market gap — §3.3), org-level progress reporting, priority support
- *Rationale:* $199 is CoachRx's 51–150 band and TeamBuildr Platinum's $200 (≤500, but no assessment system). At 150 athletes: $1.33/athlete/mo — inside the $1.50–$3.33 org band, justified by the compliance/reporting layer no mid-market competitor offers. Annual $1,990 sits squarely in the established $900–$2,800/yr org band.

#### Academy Scale — $349/mo · $3,490/yr
- Unlimited coaches · up to 400 athletes · overage $1.25/athlete/mo (CoachRx's overage rate)
- Everything in Team/Academy
- *Rationale:* Bridges TeamBuildr Platinum Pro ($280/1,000, no assessment) and Optimal Athlete ($499–$599/mo premium). 400+ athletes → custom quote, following every competitor's enterprise pattern.

#### Enterprise / District — custom quote
- 400+ athletes, multi-site, SSO/API/integrations, dedicated onboarding. Published as "Contact us" — the Teamworks/TeamBuildr pattern. Do not publish a number; enterprise buyers expect a conversation, and a published number would cap negotiation.

### 6.3 Billing & trial rules

- **Monthly and annual on every paid tier; annual = 10× monthly** (2 months free). One rule, no exceptions — simplicity is itself a differentiator vs. Trainerize's toggle confusion.
- **14-day free trial** on all coach/org tiers, no credit card (market standard). Athletes get the permanent free tier instead of a trial.
- **Active-athlete counting** (CoachRx/TrueCoach precedent): archived athletes don't count. State this plainly — it's a trust feature.
- **Payments:** Stripe pass-through (2.9% + $0.30), **0% platform fee**, advertised explicitly.
- **No setup fees** on self-serve tiers (FirstWIN's $99–$4,999 setup fees are the anti-pattern; every direct competitor is $0 setup except Trainerize's $169 branded-app fee).
- **Seasonal option for teams (consider, don't launch with):** TheBigs sells 5-month seasons. Youth sports are seasonal; a "season pass" (5 months at monthly rate, pause between) could reduce org churn. Flag for validation, not v1.

### 6.4 Sustainability sanity check (illustrative, not a forecast)

Software marginal cost per athlete is near zero; band pricing means revenue scales with *coach/org count*, not athlete count, until overages. Illustrative mix:

| Scenario | Mix | MRR | ARR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early traction | 50 Coach Starter + 20 Coach Pro + 200 Remote athletes | $1,450 + $1,580 + $2,998 = **$6,028** | ~$72k |
| Working business | 100 Starter + 60 Pro + 15 Teams + 500 Remote | $2,900 + $4,740 + $2,985 + $7,495 = **$18,120** | ~$217k |
| Scale | 200 Starter + 150 Pro + 40 Teams + 5 Scale + 1,500 Remote | $5,800 + $11,850 + $7,960 + $1,745 + $22,485 = **$49,840** | ~$598k |

The model does not require enterprise deals or per-athlete metering to work; it requires a few hundred coaches/orgs — consistent with how every competitor in §2 actually makes money. The free athlete tier is the top of funnel for all three paid segments.

## 7. Competitive positioning statement

> **Every other platform starts with programming. The Program starts with measurement.**
>
> TrainHeroic, TeamBuildr, TrueCoach, and Trainerize sell workout builders — better ways to deliver the same barbell programming. The Program sells a development system: a standardized 8-test athletic assessment, repeated every 28 days, coach-administered and coach-verified, with 130 movement games prescribed against what the assessment actually shows. No automatic prescriptions from scores — the coach always decides. The software just makes sure the decision is based on evidence, and that the athlete, parent, and coach can all see the progress.
>
> For the remote athlete: know where you stand, train what matters, prove it in 28 days.
> For the coach: sell development you can demonstrate, not workouts you can describe.
> For the academy: every athlete tested, every cycle, across every team — compliance you can see.

**One-line version:** *The assessment-driven development system for youth through adult athletes — test, train, retest, every 28 days.*

**What The Program is not:** not a workout builder competing on exercise-video count (3,000+ is table stakes it doesn't need to win), not a team-ops tool (TeamSnap's job), not an enterprise AMS (Teamworks' job), not an AI that prescribes from scores (explicitly rejected by design).
